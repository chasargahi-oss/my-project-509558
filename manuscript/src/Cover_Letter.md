# Cover letter

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief
*Journal of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition*

**Re:** Submission of a substantially revised manuscript, previously declined as JSSP-S-26-04304

Dear Editor,

We are submitting the enclosed manuscript, "Species identity outweighs native–introduced provenance in controlling soil organic carbon beneath amenity trees in a hyper-arid industrial plantation", for consideration as a new submission. The manuscript was previously declined under the number above. We have treated the decision as a request for a different paper rather than a request for corrections, and we explain below what has changed.

**What the study now claims.** The submitted version reported that six tree species planted in the South Pars industrial zone at Assaluyeh differed fourfold in soil organic carbon, and it interpreted that difference through the native-versus-introduced distinction. The revision retains the data and abandons that framing. Species identity is treated as the hypothesis of interest, provenance as a competing and confounded explanation, and the two are compared by a hierarchical partition of variance rather than by juxtaposing two R² values that differ fivefold in degrees of freedom. The result is a sharper and more defensible claim: differences among species within a provenance are about three times the differences between provenances, the carbon-richest species is also one of the two sandiest stands in the study, and the native advantage of 1.7-fold cannot be separated from the fact that native stands occupy siltier ground. We argue that the system is limited by the supply of organic matter rather than by the soil's capacity to protect it, which is why identity dominates in a way that it need not in temperate forests.

**Why we believe it is of interest beyond the site.** Industrial and amenity plantations on the Gulf coast are expanding and are irrigated with brackish water; *Conocarpus erectus* alone has been planted in its millions across the region, and it has never been evaluated for soil carbon. Our data indicate that substituting species within such a programme is a lever with a fourfold effect on concentration, that this lever does not align with the native–introduced rule of thumb on which planting decisions currently rest, and that the areal differences are large enough to matter for accounting even under a conservative bulk-density assumption. The study also extends a general result — identity dominance in soil carbon — from mesic temperate and boreal forests into an input-limited hyper-arid system on calcareous coastal soils, which is where the boundary conditions of that result needed testing.

**What has changed since the previous decision.** In response to the Editor's comments, the Introduction now states three numbered hypotheses and derives its objectives from them; the Materials and methods have been rebuilt with the subheadings the Editor requested and with the sampling date, design structure, replication unit and statistical model terms made explicit, so that the study is repeatable; the Results are organised by hypothesis, with the text carrying inference and the tables carrying values; the Discussion is a single continuous argument of nine paragraphs without subheadings, each opening with its purpose and closing with its take-home message; the Conclusions answer the hypotheses, state the mechanism and contain no citations; and all five figures have been regenerated, numbered and matched to their captions. We have also added a bulk-density sensitivity analysis (stocks at 1.2, 1.4 and 1.6 g cm^−3^), stated that the Walkley–Black determination was not corrected for incomplete oxidation and that the stocks are therefore lower bounds, and reduced the reference list from 84 entries to 39, all of which are cited.

**Declarations.** The manuscript is original, has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. Both authors have read and approved the submitted version and declare no competing interests. The study involved soil sampling only and required no ethics approval. The sample-level data are provided as Electronic Supplementary Material and will be archived with the analysis code upon acceptance. We have no financial interest in any species or product discussed, including *Conocarpus erectus*, whose performance we report unfavourably on carbon grounds.

**Suggested reviewers.** We suggest the following, none of whom has been involved in the work: Dr Bahman Ramavandi (Bushehr University of Medical Sciences; familiar with the coastal environment of Bushehr Province), Dr Tayebeh Tabatabaie (Islamic Azad University, Bushehr Branch; carbon and environmental pollution) and Dr Fazel Amiri (Islamic Azad University, Bushehr Branch; agronomy and environmental science in the study area). We would be glad to see the manuscript assessed by anyone with expertise in dryland soil carbon, and we would rather have a critical reader than a sympathetic one.

We hope the revised manuscript is now suitable for review, and we thank the Editor for comments that were specific enough to make a genuine revision possible.
