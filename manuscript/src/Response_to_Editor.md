# Response to the Editor

**Manuscript:** JSSP-S-26-04304
**Title (revised):** Species identity outweighs native–introduced provenance in controlling soil organic carbon beneath amenity trees in a hyper-arid industrial plantation
**Journal:** Journal of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition

We thank the Editor for a decision that was unusually specific and therefore unusually useful. The seven points identified a consistent underlying problem rather than seven separate ones: the manuscript reported a well-executed local survey but presented it as a general test, buried the design's logic in the methods, repeated its tables in the results, and closed with a summary instead of conclusions. We have rewritten the manuscript against that diagnosis rather than patching individual sentences. Every section has been restructured, two new analyses have been added, all figures have been regenerated and numbered, and the reference list has been reduced from 84 entries to 39 by removing citations that carried no argumentative weight.

The Editor's comments are reproduced below in italics, followed in each case by the response and by the location of the change. Line and page numbers refer to the revised manuscript.

---

**C1.** *"The novelty, originality and implication of your study are not completely clear throughout the manuscript. What progress against the most recent state-of-the-art similar studies was proposed? I strongly recommend highlighting these aspects into the abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusions. Your study seems to be local research rather than international impact one."*

**Response.** We agree that the submitted version left the reader to infer the contribution, and we have made it explicit in four places.

The novelty is not that trees differ in their effect on soil carbon — that is established for temperate and boreal forests — but where the difference resides and what that implies for a planting programme of regional scale. Three specific advances are now stated in the last paragraph of the Introduction and carried through the discussion and conclusions:

1. **First soil-carbon inventory for a hyper-arid industrial plantation on the Persian Gulf coast**, and the first evaluation of *Conocarpus erectus* — a species planted in its millions across the Gulf states over two decades and never assessed for soil carbon — against five co-occurring species.
2. **A like-for-like comparison of identity and provenance.** The submitted version compared an R² of 0.46 obtained with five degrees of freedom against an R² of 0.12 obtained with one, which is not a valid comparison and, on reflection, was the weakest inferential step in the paper. We now report a hierarchical partition in which the species sum of squares is decomposed into a provenance term (1 df) and a species-within-provenance term (4 df). The conclusion survives but in a more precise and more defensible form: variation among species within a provenance (0.34) is about three times the variation between provenances (0.12). This is a methodological contribution that transfers to any study comparing a nested categorical factor with a coarser grouping.
3. **A test of whether the provenance signal is separable from the textural differences that accompany planting history.** We quantify the confound instead of asserting a native advantage, and we state what would be required to resolve it.

The international relevance is now argued rather than assumed: the same ornamental species are planted at industrial scale across the Gulf, the same native-versus-introduced rule of thumb is used to justify that planting, and the finding that identity dominance extends from mesic forests into an input-limited hyper-arid system speaks to the boundary conditions of a general ecological result.

*Changes:* Abstract (Results and Conclusions); Introduction, final paragraph ("Three elements constitute the advance…"); Discussion, paragraphs 1 and 7; Conclusions, paragraphs 1–3. The reference list now includes the most recent comparable syntheses (Lázaro-Lobo et al. 2025; Yan et al. 2026; Ureña-Lara et al. 2026) so that the comparison with the state of the art is visible on the page.

**C2.** *"The research question is not completely clear throughout the introduction, which also lacks a clear hypothesis. Please present a hypothesis. Remember that the research objectives must result from the research hypothesis."*

**Response.** The Introduction now closes with three numbered hypotheses and with objectives that follow from them one-to-one:

- **H1:** variation in SOC among species within a provenance exceeds variation between provenances, so that species identity explains more of the total variation than provenance.
- **H2:** stands of native species hold more SOC than stands of introduced species.
- **H3:** SOC declines with depth and the decline is common to all species, that is, there is no species × depth interaction.

The objectives are then stated as the four operations needed to test them (quantify; partition; test the provenance contrast and its textural confound; convert to stocks with explicit uncertainty). Each hypothesis is tested in a named Results subsection and answered explicitly in the Conclusions, so the reader can trace the chain from question to answer.

*Changes:* Introduction, final two paragraphs; Results subsections "Species identity", "Provenance and its confounding with texture" and "Vertical distribution"; Conclusions, paragraph 1.

---

**C3.** *"Material and methods are not well explained and confusing. The methodology should be clearly developed, so that other scientists can repeat your experiment. I strongly recommend using subheadings to separate: Experimental design, treatments, specific analyses and statistical analysis."*

**Response.** The section has been rebuilt with the requested subheadings and with the operational detail that was missing:

- **Site description** — coordinates, area of the zone, catchment, geology, mean annual rainfall (157 mm) and temperature (29.9 °C), soil character and irrigation context. Much of this was previously absent and is necessary for the reader to judge transferability.
- **Experimental design and treatments** — the two factors and their levels, the nesting of species within provenance, the number of trees per species, the unit of replication, the treatment of depth as a repeated measure, and an explicit statement that this is an observational survey of established stands rather than a manipulative experiment. We also now describe the planting history that creates the provenance confound, so that the reader is not surprised by it in the Discussion.
- **Soil sampling** — sampling date (February–March 2017), increments, auger, compositing, handling and sieving.
- **Laboratory analyses** — Walkley–Black oxidation, the fact that no correction factor was applied and what that implies, the Van Bemmelen factor, hydrometer method and textural classification.
- **Bulk density and calculation of areal stocks** — the assumed value, its justification, the sensitivity calculation and the two consequences of holding it constant.
- **Statistical analysis** — transformation and its diagnostics, model terms and sums of squares, effect sizes, the pairwise procedure and compact letter display, provenance tests with bootstrap intervals, false-discovery-rate control, the mixed model and intraclass correlation, PCA and PERMANOVA, the hierarchical variance partition, power, and software with seeds.

A short statement on biological material and legislative compliance is retained as a separate subsection at the end.

*Changes:* Materials and methods, all subsections (rewritten in full).

**C4.** *"Several results are confusingly presented. Please, a systematic description of your results must be done, highlighting for the reader observations that are most relevant… In general, several paragraphs are very repetitive of the information provided in Tables and or figures."*

**Response.** The Results section has been reorganised into seven subsections that follow the order of the hypotheses, and the reporting rule has been inverted: the text now carries the inference and the tables carry the values. Where the submitted version recited all six means and all six coefficients of variation in prose, the revision states the ranking, the range and the one contrast that matters, and refers to Table 1 and Fig. 1a for the rest. Numeric repetition of tabulated values has been removed throughout; what remains in the text are effect sizes, interval estimates and test statistics that the reader needs in order to follow the argument without opening a table.

Two changes go beyond presentation. First, a genuinely confusing result has been resolved rather than merely re-described. The submitted manuscript reported that species explained 46% of multivariate dispersion and provenance 12%, and treated that as a comparison; the two terms have five and one degrees of freedom respectively, so they are not comparable. We now report the per-df values and the nested decomposition in a new Table 7, and the text explains why the naive comparison overstates the difference. Second, we added a result the earlier version missed: the species with the highest carbon concentration (*P. dactylifera*, 62.4% sand) is also one of the two sandiest, which is direct evidence against a textural explanation of the ranking and is now used in the Discussion.

*Changes:* Results, subsections "Soil properties and the species ranking" through "Robustness and sensitivity"; new Table 7; Figs. 1–5.

**C5.** *"The discussion section should be improved. I suggest removing subheadings… The discussion should link all of your findings… each paragraph should be well structured and interconnected ("telling a story")… begin with a sentence that introduces what the paragraph is about, link their findings with those in the literature, and finish with what are the main points… Avoid repeat results. The novelty and implication of your study should also be highlighted throughout this section."*

**Response.** The discussion is now a single continuous section of eight paragraphs with no subheadings, and each paragraph is built to the structure the Editor describes: a topic sentence that states what the paragraph does, the link to the literature, and a closing sentence that carries the take-home message. The sequence is: (i) the central finding and why it matters; (ii) why provenance fails as a predictor, because it is a label and not a trait; (iii) the mechanism proposed for *P. dactylifera* and why it is testable; (iv) the *C. erectus* result and the stratification-ratio artefact it exposes; (v) the carbon–clay relationship and the input-limitation argument; (vi) the provenance confound and what would falsify the inference; (vii) the vertical structure and the within-species heterogeneity; (viii) novelty, implications and limitations.

Results are not restated except where a number is needed to carry an argument, and the novelty and implications appear in paragraphs 1, 7 and 8 rather than being held back for the conclusions.

*Changes:* Discussion (rewritten in full; all subheadings removed).

**C6.** *"The conclusions section should not be a summary of your study or an extension of the discussion. This section should illustrate the mechanistic links of findings… should answer the hypothesis… focus on the implication… bibliographic citations must not be mentioned in this section."*

**Response.** The Conclusions have been rewritten to answer the three hypotheses in order, to state the mechanism in one paragraph (input limitation rather than mineral protection governs the pool, which is why traits rather than origin determine the outcome), and to close on the implication for plantation design. Two citations appeared in the previous conclusions (Culqui et al. 2025; Cha et al. 2019) and both have been removed; the section now contains none.

*Changes:* Conclusions (rewritten; 0 citations).

**C7.** *"Matching the figure legends to the corresponding figures is confusing because the figures themselves are not labeled with their respective figure numbers. Please clearly identify each figure."*

**Response.** All figures have been regenerated at 300 dpi, each is now a separate file numbered in its filename (`Figure1.png` … `Figure5.png`), each carries a numbered caption placed directly beneath it, and the panels are labelled (a) and (b) consistently with the caption text. The submitted version combined several figures into single composite image files, which is what caused the mismatch; that is no longer the case. Each figure is also called out in the text in the order in which it appears.

Three further changes to the figures are worth flagging. The box plots of individual samples in the original Fig. 1 have been replaced by means with standard deviations, which is what the available summary data support; the original distributional detail is retained in Table 1 (range and CV) and can be restored from the sample-level data in Table S1 if the Editor prefers. A new figure (Fig. 4) shows the bulk-density sensitivity of the stocks. The PCA loading plot (Fig. 5) replaces the original biplot, because sample scores and loadings were not separable in the composite image.

*Changes:* Figs. 1–5 (regenerated); all figure callouts in the text renumbered consistently; Figure captions section.

### Additional changes made at our own initiative

These were not requested but became necessary once the manuscript was re-examined.

1. **Bulk-density sensitivity.** Stocks are now reported at 1.2, 1.4 and 1.6 g cm^−3^ (Table 8, Fig. 4). The species ranking is invariant and the absolute range widens to 17.6–112.5 Mg C ha^−1^. This makes the dominant uncertainty visible instead of hiding it in a limitation.
2. **Oxidation correction.** We now state that no correction factor was applied to the Walkley–Black determination, so concentrations and stocks are conservative lower bounds. The submitted version was silent on this point.
3. **Nested variance partition and per-df comparison** (C4, above).
4. **Reference list.** Reduced from 84 to 39 entries, all of which are cited in the text; the remaining citations carried no argument. Reference details were checked and one entry that lacked volume and page numbers (Lázaro-Lobo et al. 2025) was completed.
5. **Data and code availability.** Table S1 with the sample-level record is submitted with the manuscript; the analysis is fully scripted and the scripts will be archived with the data.
6. **Language.** The manuscript has been edited throughout for concision and for consistent British spelling, and hedging that was not supported by the design has been removed.
